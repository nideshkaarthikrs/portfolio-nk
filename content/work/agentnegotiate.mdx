## The problem

Small teams that buy the same categories of goods and services repeatedly — packaging, raw
materials, software seats — spend a disproportionate amount of time on back-and-forth
negotiation for purchases that follow the same pattern every time. Someone has to chase quotes,
push back on price, and stay inside a budget, and none of it gets recorded anywhere useful.

Procurement tools either skip negotiation entirely (fixed-price catalogs) or leave it to email
threads with no policy enforcement and no audit trail. There's no middle layer that negotiates
on your behalf while staying inside rules you actually trust.

A policy engine sitting between both agents that evaluates every offer in real time and blocks
anything that violates the configured floor, ceiling, or merchant terms — before it ever reaches
settlement.

<PlaceholderVisual label="Audit log — screenshot coming soon" className="aspect-video w-full" />

A full audit trail: every offer, counter, block, and the final agreed price, written to an
append-only log so a human can reconstruct exactly how a deal was reached.

Payment settlement wired to the agreed price once both sides confirm, so a negotiated deal turns
into a completed transaction without a manual handoff.

## Architecture

**Key decisions and trade-offs**

- **Policy engine as a separate service, not agent logic.** Keeping spend limits and merchant
  rules out of the agent's own reasoning means a policy violation is a deterministic block, not
  something an LLM could be argued out of. The trade-off is an extra network hop on every offer.
- **Every message is logged before it's acted on.** The audit log writes synchronously ahead of
  settlement, which adds latency to each round but means there's never a settled deal without a
  matching trail.
- **Agents negotiate in structured offers, not free text.** Constraining the protocol to typed
  offer objects makes the policy engine's job simple and makes replays (like the one above)
  possible, at the cost of a less "natural" negotiation transcript.

## Where it's going

Next milestones: a merchant-side policy editor so counterparties can configure their own floor
and terms without touching code, and a real Stripe-backed settlement flow in place of the
current sandboxed one. Multi-round negotiation across more than two agents is on the roadmap but
not started.
